Heave simulator

A rig that moves the drone dock up and down like a boat deck so we can test landings in the lab. The spec from Vasu Kaker, who leads the dock project, was 1.5 m of travel at 0.8 m/s. It's built to carry the lab's pitch-roll platform and is sized for a 15 kg payload, 30 kg at most.

MIT Beaver Works, October 2026 to present. Built and running.

Design

Lead screws and ball screws were out. A 1.7 m screw goes unstable (whips) near 1,000 rpm, and an 8 mm lead needs 6,000 rpm to reach 0.8 m/s. Rod actuators are too slow. I went with a belt drive. Two towers hold a carriage between them, one motor on a jackshaft drives both belts, and each tower has a counterweight.

With the counterweights, peak motor torque for the 15 kg payload drops from 4.40 to 0.96 N·m. The motor is a closed-loop NEMA 34 stepper with a power-off brake on a 2:1 belt reduction. The brake stops the carriage from full speed in 127 mm. Parts list: $2,444 of a $2,500 cap.

Changes before ordering

  • Raised belt pretension from 150 N to 250 N. At 150 N one span could go slack during a landing or a brake stop.
  • Added a relay so a driver fault also sets the brake.
  • Side-to-side natural frequency came out at 5.1 Hz, barely above the 5 Hz minimum, so I added gussets at the tower bases.

Safety

The rig has soft limits in firmware, overtravel switches on a hard-wired E-stop chain, the brake, and shock absorbers at both ends of travel. Pins lock the carriage when the payload is being changed.
