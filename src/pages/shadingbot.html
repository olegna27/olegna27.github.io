ShadingBot

Final project for MIT 6.4210 Robotic Manipulation, with Alexander Li and Laura Zhang. A simulated KUKA iiwa in Drake shades a grayscale image by pressing a soft brush harder where the image is darker. We compare three ways of choosing the pressure: a calibration table with no feedback, camera feedback, and force sensing only.

Fall 2026. Simulation only.

{{vid:shadingbot/compare-tilt.mp4|shadingbot/summary-tilt.png|Three simulated runs drawing the same step wedge on a canvas tilted two degrees}}

The same target drawn with each method on a canvas tilted 2°.

I built the Drake scene, the three methods, the tests and CI, and a small app that runs the methods side by side.

The force-only method isn't told where the canvas is. At the start of each row it presses down, fits the surface height and the brush stiffness from the force curve, and then holds a force.

Changes along the way

  • The camera moved from overhead to the front of the canvas. In my visibility check the arm blocked the overhead view of the brush completely.
  • The canvas moved 4 cm so the arm could reach every point with the brush tilted.
  • The first camera loop only watched the center of each row and did no better than the table. It improved after I widened it to the full row and fixed a touchdown bug.

The paint model's pressure limits are placeholders, and the table and camera methods command depth while the force method commands force, so the comparison isn't fully even yet.
